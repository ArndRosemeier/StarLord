# Diplomacy by the original rules

- Relations now work the way the classic game's do. Each pair of empires shares one relation that starts where the two races' history puts it and slowly drifts back there. Treaties warm it a little each turn, and it can't go above 65 without an alliance.
- Events count for more when relations are poor and for less when they are already good. Honorable rulers take offence twice as hard, and charismatic races' good deeds count double.
- War drops relations to between -76 and -100. Peace lifts them by 50, but no higher than 0, and stops either side declaring war for 30 turns. After that the peace treaty lapses.
- Trade and research treaties pay half the smaller empire's population, times your government's rate, with +50% trade for Fantastic Traders. They start in the red and build up over about ten turns, and a research treaty's setup cost is paid in BC.
- Computer rulers keep a tally of strikes against you for spying, threatening fleets and harsh demands. The first strike is a warning; a third can mean war if their fleets can stand up to yours.
- Computer empires sign treaties and fight wars with each other, and allies join each other's wars. They no longer attack you just because you are close by or doing well.
- Computer rulers answer demands by weighing your fleets, size and technology against their own. They may give in, refuse, warn you, end your treaties or declare war.
- The council gives one vote per 10 population, rounded up. Voters lean toward the candidate they backed last time and remember who voted against them. If you refuse a computer winner, every other computer empire joins it against you.
- On Hard and Impossible, computer empires you have no treaty with grow jealous of your treaties with others. Difficulty no longer simply makes them dislike you.
- The Diplomacy screen shows each relation's baseline, its chance to drift, treaty warmth and any strikes against you.
