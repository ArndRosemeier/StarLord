# Galaxies made the classic way

- New galaxies follow the original's generator: 20, 36, 54 or 71 stars on maps of 17 × 13, 25 × 20, 34 × 27 and 51 × 40 parsecs, spread over an even grid so names never overlap.
- Galaxy age now sets the classic star mix: young galaxies are full of blue and white stars, old ones of yellow and orange stars with more fertile middle orbits. Age no longer changes minerals, and neither do nebulae.
- Planets follow star color, orbit and zone: inner orbits are scorched, outer ones frozen, gas giants never sit in the innermost orbit, and brown dwarfs sometimes have a planet. Inferno worlds no longer appear.
- Gravity follows the classic table: rich worlds are dense, small poor worlds light.
- Black holes stand at least 5 parsecs from any other star.
- About a third of systems with planets hold a special: natives are the most common, then ancient artifacts and wormholes. A wormhole's two ends are always far apart.
- Your home star keeps its natural color, your system has at least three real planets, a system with a real planet lies within 4 parsecs, and a planet that can grow food within 6 (unless your people eat rocks). Homes are spread far apart.
- Orion sits near the middle of the map: a huge, ultra rich Gaia world with normal gravity among asteroid belts.
- Monster lairs are rarer (more on Hard and Impossible, at most 20), hold a random monster, guard an upgraded rich planet and keep the system's own special.
- Average starts know the six basic fields and nothing more; pre-warp starts only Engineering. Advanced starts learn 19 more fields, begin with 200 BC (more or less with the money pick) and a homeworld at 60% of its population.
- Big maps can be zoomed out to show the whole galaxy.
